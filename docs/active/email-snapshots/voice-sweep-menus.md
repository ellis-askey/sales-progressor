## MENU FOR CLUSTER 1 — Closings

Current state: ~24 bodies close on "We'll let you know X" / "We'll be in touch" / "you'll hear from us" / "We'll keep you posted." Reader hits this register on essentially every email; the closing has stopped registering, and high-stakes moments (exchange, completion) can't get the weight they deserve because the same phrase carries every email.

Goal: each closing fitted to its specific moment, with the menu of constructions varied as a set. Many bodies just don't need a closer — that's actively part of the menu.

### The 8 constructions

**A. Time-bound forward-look — naming the specific next event.** Used SPARINGLY — only when there's a real binary next moment the reader cares about. Each instance names the specific event, not "we'll let you know."

Pattern shape: "[Specific event] will follow within [timeframe]." / "The next milestone on this trail is [specific event]." / "The next email on this file will likely be [specific event]."

Best fit: 3–4 instances total. PM23 vendor (post cluster-9 fix), VM18 vendor, PM25 purchaser — the three "exchange-is-imminent" bodies. Maybe PM24 vendor or PM24 purchaser, not both.

**B. Drop entirely — body ends on its substantive paragraph.** No closer. The substantive content carries closure on its own.

Pattern shape: just don't add a "we'll let you know" sentence.

Best fit: 6–8 instances. VM11 vendor + purchaser, VM14 vendor + purchaser, PM23 purchaser (one paragraph already does the work), PM24 purchaser (similar), several others where the substantive paragraph is already the natural ending.

**C. Watchful conditional — solicitor surfaces material.** Moves the agent of follow-up from the platform to the solicitor; lets the reader know follow-up is event-driven, not scheduled.

Pattern shape: "Your solicitor will surface anything material once they've worked through it." / "If anything material comes up, your solicitor will flag it." / "Expect them to come to you if your input is needed."

Best fit: 4–5 instances. PM15 purchaser, VM12 vendor (the long "if anything's left open" paragraph), PM7 vendor mid-sentence (the "if so" tail), PM8 vendor, VM7 vendor.

**D. Hand-off to specific other party for the actual follow-up.** Names who's coming back to the reader and what about. Not "we'll be in touch" — "your solicitor/broker/surveyor will."

Pattern shape: "Your solicitor will be in touch separately about [thing]." / "Your broker will flag if [trigger]." / "The surveyor will follow up directly about access."

Best fit: 3–4 instances. PM22 purchaser deposit (already exists in this register), PM16 mortgage variant (already moves toward this), PM10 vendor.

**E. Definitive closure — end-of-arc.** No follow-up expected; the milestone IS the closure. Voice carries the warmth or finality of the moment.

Pattern shape: variable — fitted to the closing moment itself, not a template.

Best fit: 3 instances. PM20 (already in this register: "the file's tempo typically picks up once the enquiries phase is closed"), PM27 (already in this register: "Thank you for letting us help you through it"), VM20 (similar).

**F. Time-anchored later moment — for specific upcoming windows.** Anchors the reader to a specific future trigger, not a generic "watch this space."

Pattern shape: "Closer to [date/event], expect [specific kind of email] with [specific details]." / "When [trigger], we'll send through [thing]."

Best fit: 2–3 instances. VM19 vendor (completion-day specifics email), PM26 purchaser (completion-day practical specifics), maybe VM6 vendor (next milestone in the chain).

**G. Stand-by / runs in background — no scheduled follow-up.** Tells the reader nothing is expected and the file moves on its own clock. Active register of "no action needed" without saying it.

Pattern shape: "This one runs in the background while [other thread] progresses." / "The file moves on its own clock now." / "Your next active step is [X]."

Best fit: 3–4 instances. PM8 purchaser (already in this register after cluster-6 fix), PM14 purchaser (similar), VM4 purchaser, possibly PM5 purchaser.

**H. Reverse the assumption — "The next time you hear from us…"** Reframes the closer as marking what the NEXT email will be about, giving the closer some bite.

Pattern shape: "The next time you hear from us, [X] will have happened." / "Look out for the next email on this trail — it'll be when [Y] confirms."

Best fit: 2–3 instances. PM25 purchaser (the "you'll hear from us the second it happens" body — could be reframed here), VM18 vendor.

### Distribution math

24 bodies, 8 constructions:

- A (forward-look): 3 instances
- B (drop): 7 instances
- C (watchful): 4 instances
- D (hand-off): 3 instances
- E (closure): 3 instances
- F (time-anchored): 2 instances
- G (stand-by): 4 instances
- H (reframe): 2 instances

Total: 28 placements for 24 bodies — slight over-count because some bodies will use a combination (e.g. C in one paragraph + B at the end). Workable.

## MENU FOR CLUSTER 8 — Leasehold paragraph openers

Current state: ~14 leasehold-conditional paragraphs across the corpus, the vast majority opening "On a leasehold sale like this…" or "On a leasehold purchase like this…" Two existing variants ("One thing worth flagging on the leasehold side…", "One thing to flag on a leasehold purchase like this…") are also same-template-different-costume — they all share the structure of an introductory leasehold-flag clause that hands off to the substance.

Goal per your note: **structurally different constructions, not synonym variants.** "On a leasehold like this" and "Because the property's leasehold" really are the same template in different costumes. The alternates need different sentence shapes.

### The 5 structurally distinct shapes

**Shape α — Artifact-led.** Lead with the leasehold-specific noun (the management pack, the TA7 form, service charges) as the grammatical subject. The leasehold framing is implicit through the artifact's name; no introductory clause needed.

Pattern: "The management pack from your freeholder is on its own clock — typically 2–6 weeks…"

Pattern: "The TA7 leasehold information form joins TA6 and TA10 in this pack — that's the one covering the lease itself…"

Best fit: 5–6 instances. The longest-running paragraphs that explain what the leasehold artifact IS. VM1 vendor + purchaser, VM2 vendor, VM6 vendor, VM7 vendor (TA7), PM2 purchaser, PM7 purchaser, VM7 purchaser slim.

**Shape β — Imperative / advisory direct.** Open with a verb directly addressing the reader. The leasehold framing is in the action being asked for.

Pattern: "Keep an eye on the management pack timeline — it comes from the freeholder on its own clock, and it's the most common bottleneck on leasehold sales."

Pattern: "Watch for the management pack arrival — leasehold sales tend to slow there if it stalls."

Best fit: 2–3 instances. Milestones where the reader can do something useful (chase the freeholder, brace for the wait). VM6 vendor (the "chase yourself" tone fits), VM1 vendor.

**Shape γ — Existential / additive.** "There's also…" or "There's a leasehold-specific piece…" — names the additional thing without leading with a leasehold flag.

Pattern: "There's also the management pack to factor in — coming from the freeholder on its own clock and a common source of delay on leasehold sales."

Pattern: "There's a leasehold-specific piece running alongside the standard pack — the freeholder's management pack…"

Best fit: 2 instances. Buyer-side heads-ups where the leasehold scope is being added to the broader summary. VM3 vendor LL, PM1 purchaser.

**Shape δ — Time-clause-first / subordinate.** Lead with a temporal subordinate clause that frames when/while the leasehold piece is happening.

Pattern: "While the contract pack is being prepared, the management pack from your freeholder is moving on its own track — that piece typically takes 2–6 weeks…"

Pattern: "While that's running, the leasehold-specific piece — the management pack from the freeholder — is on its own timeline."

Best fit: 2 instances. Bodies where the leasehold piece is concurrent with another thread. VM2 vendor, VM10 vendor.

**Shape ε — Pronoun-led / "It's worth knowing…" register, kept tight.** Already used by PM1 purchaser ("One thing worth flagging…") and VM2 purchaser ("One thing to flag…"). Worth keeping as one of the shapes since it does a specific job — a slim "heads-up note" register without lots of detail. But cap at 2–3 instances total, not 10.

Pattern: "Worth knowing on the leasehold side: [the substance]."

Pattern: "One thing to flag on the leasehold side — [the substance]."

Best fit: 2–3 instances. Slim flag-style mentions where the leasehold piece is a footnote rather than a substantive add-on. PM1 purchaser (already in register), VM2 purchaser (already), maybe PM14 vendor slim nudge.

### Distribution math

14 instances, 5 shapes:

- α (artifact-led): 5–6 instances
- β (imperative): 2–3 instances
- γ (existential): 2 instances
- δ (time-clause): 2 instances
- ε (worth-knowing): 2–3 instances

Total: 13–16 placements for ~14 bodies. Works.

### The "On a leasehold sale like this" construction itself

NOT on the menu. Every instance currently using this construction gets rewritten to one of α–ε. That's the point of the cluster-8 work.
