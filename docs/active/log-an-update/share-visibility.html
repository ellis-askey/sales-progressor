Who actually sees a “shared” update

Before we build a “who sees this” control, here is exactly what happens today when you flick Share with client on a logged call, email or text — traced from the live portal code, not assumed. The short version: it was never “everyone,” and one case is quietly broken.

01 The one control you have today

When you log a communication, you tick who it was with and flick a single Share with client switch. There is no separate “audience” setting — the people you tick are the audience.

→There is no per-person “can see this” flag. Visibility is this one boolean. The audience is inferred from contactIds and then re-filtered by side when the portal loads.

02 The rule the portal actually applies

On read, the portal shows a shared comm only if it’s visibleToClient and it passes a side check tied to who you ticked. The two sides are treated differently.

Buyer view A buyer sees it only if their own name is in contactIds (and it’s for their active round). Tick Tom, and only Tom sees it — not a second buyer you didn’t tick.
Seller view A seller sees it if it was addressed to any seller contact, or if it has no contacts at all (a file-wide broadcast).

So in the example above — shared, ticked to Tom the buyer — Tom sees it; Jane the seller does not. It was never “all clients.”

A comm shared with no one ticked reaches the seller but is invisible to every buyer — because the buyer check needs their name in the list and the seller check treats an empty list as a broadcast. That mismatch is a genuine bug, not a design choice.

05 The real gap

Two problems fall out of coupling “who it was with” to “who can see it.”

Friction You can’t aim it

To show the seller a summary of a call you had with the buyer, you’d have to tick the seller as a participant of that call — misrepresenting who you actually spoke to. Audience and participants are the same field, so you can’t keep the record honest and choose the audience.

Bug “Everyone” drops buyers

The most intuitive action — share with no one specific, meaning “tell both sides” — reaches sellers and silently skips every buyer. Nobody would predict that from the switch, and there’s no warning.

06 What I’d propose

Give sharing its own small control, separate from the participant list, defaulting to the obvious side so the common case stays one tap.

Proposed — when Share is on

Who sees this

→Defaults to the side(s) of whoever you ticked as participants, so it’s zero extra effort normally. Widen to both sides, or narrow to named people, when you want to. And “both sides” genuinely means both — fixing the broadcast bug on the way.
