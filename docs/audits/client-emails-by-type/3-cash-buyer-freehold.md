# Client milestone emails — Cash buyer · Freehold

Every client-facing confirmation email that sends on a **Cash buyer · Freehold** sale, in timeline order. Seller (vendor) and Buyer (purchaser) only — internal copies are excluded.

**How to use this for a copy review:**

- Improve only the text of the five labelled fields (Subject, Hero label, Opening, What happened, What next).
- Leave the headings (e.g. `### PM9 — Book your survey · Buyer`) exactly as they are — they tell me where each rewrite belongs.
- Leave `«placeholders»` untouched — those are live values dropped in at send time (property address, dates, surveyor firm).
- Where a block says the wording is *identical across every transaction type*, the same string is reused everywhere, so improve it once and I'll apply it everywhere.

**Not sent on this transaction type:** PM5, PM6, PM11, VM8, VM9, PM12 — management-pack steps (VM8, VM9, PM12) are leasehold-only; mortgage steps (PM5, PM6, PM11) are mortgage-only.

## VM1 — Instruct your solicitor

### VM1 — Instruct your solicitor · Seller

_Wording: identical across every transaction type that sends this email._

- **Subject:** You've instructed your solicitor: «property address»
- **Hero label:** Solicitor instructed
- **Opening:** Your solicitor is now instructed and can start getting things underway.
- **What happened:** You've formally instructed your solicitor to act on the sale. They'll now start the conveyancing process, which involves preparing the contract pack, gathering title documents, and handling any questions that come in from the buyer's solicitor.
- **What next:** Your solicitor will prepare the contract pack and, if the property is leasehold, request the management pack from your freeholder or managing agent. This typically takes a few weeks. We'll keep you updated as things move on.

### VM1 — Instruct your solicitor · Buyer

_Wording: identical across every transaction type that sends this email._

- **Subject:** The seller has instructed their solicitor: «property address»
- **Hero label:** Seller's solicitor instructed
- **Opening:** A little update on where things are with your purchase.
- **What happened:** The seller has formally instructed their solicitor to act on the sale. This is an important early step. Things are now moving on the seller's side of the transaction.
- **What next:** There's nothing you need to do for this step at the moment. The seller's solicitor will prepare the contract pack and send it to your solicitor in the coming weeks. We'll let you know when that happens.

### PM9 — Book your survey · Buyer

_Wording: **varies by transaction type.** This version is used for: Cash buyer · Freehold; Cash buyer · Leasehold; Cash from sale proceeds · Freehold; Cash from sale proceeds · Leasehold. Other transaction types word this email differently._

- **Subject:** Your survey is arranged, «property address»
- **Hero label:** Survey arranged
- **Opening:** Your property survey has been arranged with «surveyor firm».
- **What happened:** The surveyor will inspect the property in person and produce a report directly for you. A Level 2 (HomeBuyer) survey typically takes 1 to 2 hours at the property, while a Level 3 (full structural) survey can take 2 to 3 hours.
- **What next:** You can usually expect to receive the report within a week or two of the survey. Once you have it, take some time to read through it carefully. If anything significant is highlighted, it's worth speaking with your surveyor before deciding what to do next. They'll be able to explain whether a finding is something that needs attention or is fairly routine for a property of this type and age.

### PM10 — Survey report received · Buyer

_Wording: identical across every transaction type that sends this email._

- **Subject:** Your survey report is in, «property address»
- **Hero label:** Survey report received
- **Opening:** Your survey report is now available.
- **What happened:** Take some time to read through it carefully. Surveyors generally use condition ratings to help highlight which areas need the most attention, so focus particularly on anything given a higher-risk rating or identified as requiring further investigation. If there's anything you don't understand or you're unsure how significant it is, speak with your surveyor before deciding what to do next. They'll usually be happy to talk through their findings and explain whether something is a genuine concern or a fairly routine observation for a property of this type and age.
- **What next:** If the report highlights anything you feel needs to be raised with the seller, such as a significant defect, further information or documentation, speak with your solicitor. It's worth deciding what you'd actually like clarified or resolved first, and your solicitor can then raise the appropriate enquiries with the seller's side.
